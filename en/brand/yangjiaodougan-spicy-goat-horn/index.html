Brand Story

Yangjiao Dried Tofu originated in Yangjiao Town, Wulong District, Chongqing, with a history dating back to the late Qing Dynasty (around the late 19th century). Legend has it that a local tofu maker surnamed Wang accidentally discovered that pressing the tofu dry and then braising it with spices like Sichuan peppercorn and chili created a unique flavor. He gradually developed a distinctive technique, which was passed down through generations of the Wang family, who continuously refined the process, making Yangjiao Dried Tofu widely renowned. By the early 20th century, Yangjiao Town had become an important trading hub in the Sichuan-Chongqing region, and Yangjiao Dried Tofu gained fame as a local specialty among traveling merchants. The craft involves selecting high-quality soybeans and going through multiple steps including soaking, grinding, filtering, coagulating, pressing, and braising. The final product is golden in color, delicate in texture, and rich in spicy and savory flavors. More than just a food, Yangjiao Dried Tofu carries the wisdom and dietary culture of the Bayu people, serving as a local delicacy and a symbol of hospitality. Today, it is listed in the Chongqing Municipal Intangible Cultural Heritage, representing a culinary icon of Chongqing.

Cultural Note

The production of Yangjiao Dried Tofu follows traditional handcraft methods, including bean selection, soaking, grinding, filtering, boiling, coagulating, pressing, and braising. The braising step is crucial, requiring a blend of various spices and Chinese herbs, with the heat and timing mastered through experience. This craft reflects the wisdom of folk food processing in the Bayu region and holds significant local cultural value.
