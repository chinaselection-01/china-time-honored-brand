Brand Story

Hongmei brand traces its origins to Liaoning, with a history dating back to the mid-to-late Qing Dynasty. According to local chronicles, during the Jiaqing reign (1796-1820), workshops in the Shenyang area produced pickled vegetables and condiments, among which the 'Hongmei' name was most famous. The founder (name unknown) pickled vegetables using a secret family recipe, prized for their bright red color and crisp texture. During the Tongzhi reign (1862-1874), Hongmei products were selected as local tribute items, gaining widespread fame. In the Republic era, Hongmei established branches in Shenyang, Dalian, and other cities, becoming a key food supplier in Northeast China. After the founding of New China, Hongmei was integrated into a state-owned food factory, continuing its traditional brewing techniques. Hongmei not only represents food but also embodies the deep affection of Liaoning people for hometown flavors, serving as an important symbol of Northeast culinary culture.

Cultural Note

Hongmei preserves the traditional techniques of Northeast Chinese pickled vegetables and condiments, involving steps such as ingredient selection, washing, pickling, and fermentation, with an emphasis on natural fermentation and seasonal variations. Hongmei products are not just daily seasonings but also reflect the wisdom of Liaoning people in food preservation and flavor, forming a vital part of local food culture.
