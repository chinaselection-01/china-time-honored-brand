Brand Story

Yuhe, a jewelry brand originating from Hunan, has a history that is not officially documented, but local chronicles and trade anecdotes suggest it may trace back to the mid-to-late Qing Dynasty. During that period, cities like Changsha and Xiangtan flourished in commerce, and the jade and jewelry trade began to thrive. The name 'Yuhe' combines 'yu' (jade), symbolizing warmth and virtue, with 'he' (harmony), reflecting the brand's philosophy of carrying virtue through jade and passing harmony through family. Early artisans, mostly from hereditary local families, excelled in carving and setting jadeite and white jade, creating pieces that embodied the simplicity and delicacy of Huxiang culture. Although no specific founder is recorded, Yuhe appeared in local chamber of commerce directories during the Republic of China era, possibly linked to Hunan merchant families dealing in jewelry. After the founding of New China, Yuhe underwent public-private partnerships and market reforms, preserving its craftsmanship but limiting its influence to the region. Culturally, Yuhe represents one of Hunan's native jewelry traditions, carrying forward the lineage of Huxiang handicraft and reflecting local commercial and aesthetic evolution.

Cultural Note

Yuhe's craftsmanship focuses on traditional jade carving, emphasizing material selection and hand polishing, with a principle of 'crafting according to the material'. Its setting techniques incorporate elements of Xiang embroidery, showcasing local cultural characteristics. However, specific technical details lack public historical records, and some techniques may have been lost, with modern designs now taking precedence.
