Brand Story

Originating in Jiangsu, the Wang brand's exact founding year remains unverified officially. According to local chronicles and oral history, the Wang family operated a food workshop in Jiangsu around the mid-Qing Dynasty (circa 1800), specializing in pastries and pickled vegetables. The founder, an ancestor of the Wang family, was meticulous in selecting ingredients and skilled in handcrafting, producing pastries that were soft and sweet, and pickles that were crisp and refreshing, gradually earning the admiration of neighbors. By the late Qing and early Republic period, descendants expanded the business, opening shops in Suzhou and Wuxi, establishing the 'Wang' brand. Adhering to the motto 'Real ingredients, traditional handcrafting,' the brand passed down through generations. During the War of Resistance, the Wang family supported the front lines with food, demonstrating patriotic spirit. After the public-private partnership in 1956, the Wang brand merged into local food factories, but traditional recipes and techniques were preserved. Today, 'Wang' stands as a symbol of local time-honored heritage, carrying the culinary memories of Jiangnan, with its pastries and pickles remaining beloved home-style delicacies, embodying the refinement and simplicity of Su-style cuisine.

Cultural Note

Wang's craftsmanship is primarily manual, with pastries emphasizing 'fine flour, pure sugar, and precise heat,' while pickles follow 'sun-drying and dew-exposure, natural fermentation.' The process reflects Jiangnan people's respect for seasons and ingredients, serving as a living inheritance of Su-style food culture.
