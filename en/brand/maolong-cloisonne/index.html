Brand Story

Founded in the late Qing Dynasty and early Republic of China period, Maolong's exact founding year remains unverified. Its name, derived from 'Maosheng Longchang,' symbolizes prosperity and thriving business. The founder, whose name is yet to be confirmed, opened a shop in Beijing specializing in traditional crafts such as jewelry, porcelain, and lacquerware. Maolong quickly gained a reputation for exquisite craftsmanship and honest business practices, attracting the favor of imperial nobles and literati. In the early 20th century, its products were exported overseas, serving as a bridge for cultural exchange between China and the West. Through a century of vicissitudes, Maolong has upheld traditional techniques while integrating modern aesthetics, becoming a gem among China's time-honored brands. Its cultural significance lies in inheritance and innovation, showcasing the excellence and inclusiveness of Chinese craftsmanship.

Cultural Note

Maolong's craftsmanship is inherited from the imperial workshop (Zaobanchu), blending styles such as Beijing-style (Jingzuo) and Suzhou-style (Suzuo). It emphasizes the perfect combination of materials and techniques. Its products not only have practical value but also carry traditional aesthetics and royal grandeur, making them outstanding representatives of Chinese handicraft.
