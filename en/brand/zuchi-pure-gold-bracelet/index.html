Brand Story

Zuchi, a jewelry brand from Gansu, takes its name from the Chinese term for pure gold, symbolizing absolute purity and sincerity. Gansu, a key hub on the ancient Silk Road, has long been a crossroads of Eastern and Western trade and culture, where jewelry-making techniques flourished and evolved. Historical records indicate that during the Ming and Qing dynasties (1368-1911), goldsmith shops in Lanzhou and other cities handcrafted pure gold ornaments for weddings and religious ceremonies. In modern times, Gansu's goldsmiths have preserved ancient methods while incorporating Western styles, creating a distinctive regional character. Although Zuchi's exact founding year is not documented, its craftsmanship lineage can be traced to the goldsmith communities of central Gansu during the Qing dynasty (1644-1911). The brand emphasizes material purity and superior workmanship, embodying the northwestern people's commitment to integrity and quality. Today, Zuchi is dedicated to reviving traditional goldsmithing techniques, infusing them with modern design to give new life to ancient crafts, and has become a cultural ambassador for Gansu's jewelry heritage.

Cultural Note

Zuchi inherits Gansu's traditional gold and silver fine craftsmanship, including smelting, hammering, chasing, and filigree work. The craft emphasizes 'pure gold' quality and meticulous handwork, reflecting the simplicity and rigor of northwestern artisans. The brand's culture blends the commercial spirit of the Silk Road with folk auspicious beliefs, and its products are often used for weddings, festivals, and heirlooms, carrying strong regional folk characteristics.
