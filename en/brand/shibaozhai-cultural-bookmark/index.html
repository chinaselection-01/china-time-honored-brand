Brand Story

Shibaozhai, located in Shibao Town, Zhongxian County, Chongqing, on the northern bank of the Yangtze River, was first built during the Wanli reign of the Ming Dynasty (1573-1620). The solitary peak rising abruptly from the river resembles a jade seal, hence the name 'Shibao' (stone treasure). Legend has it that to honor Guan Yu, a famed general of the Three Kingdoms period, locals built a temple dedicated to him at the summit, which later evolved into a complex blending Confucianism, Buddhism, and Taoism. During the Kangxi reign of the Qing Dynasty (1662-1722), craftsmen constructed a nine-story pavilion along the cliff for easier ascent, and during the Guangxu reign (1875-1908), three more stories were added, forming a twelve-story tower reaching 56 meters in height. Entirely of wood and without a single nail, it is an architectural marvel. Shibaozhai not only embodies the wisdom of ancient craftsmen but also serves as a vital cultural landmark of the Three Gorges region, witnessing the prosperity of river shipping and the continuity of folk beliefs since the Ming and Qing dynasties. Its unique 'fortress-mountain' integration has earned it the title 'Pearl on the River,' and it was designated a National Key Cultural Relic Protection Unit in 2001.

Cultural Note

The architectural techniques of Shibaozhai combine chuandou (column-and-tie) and tailiang (post-and-lintel) wooden structures, with multi-layered eaves that create a majestic appearance, exemplifying the high level of construction in eastern Sichuan during the Ming and Qing dynasties. The fortress preserves numerous stone inscriptions, couplets, and murals from these periods, serving as important material evidence for studying the religion, folklore, and architectural history of the Bayu region. Its design philosophy of 'adapting to the mountain's contours with ingenious craftsmanship' offers insights for modern architecture to coexist harmoniously with nature.
