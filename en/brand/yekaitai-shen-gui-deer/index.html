Brand Story

Founded in 1637 during the Ming Dynasty by Ye Wenji, Yekaitai began as a modest herbal pharmacy in Hankou. Under the Qing Dynasty, Ye Hongliang moved the business to Dajia Street, officially naming it 'Yekaitai.' By the Daoguang era, it had become one of Wuhan's most renowned medicine shops, ranking alongside Beijing's Tongrentang, Hangzhou's Huqingyutang, and Guangzhou's Chen Liji as one of China's Four Great Medicine Houses. Yekaitai upheld the principles of 'devout preparation and fair dealing,' sourcing premium herbs and following ancient processing methods. Its signature remedies, such as Shengui Lurong Wan and Babao Oil, gained fame both at home and abroad. During the War of Resistance, Yekaitai donated medicines to soldiers, embodying a deep sense of patriotism. After 1956, it merged into Wuhan Jianmin Pharmaceutical, yet its legacy endures.

Signature Products

Shengui Lurong Wan

A refined pill made from ginseng, deer antler, cinnamon, and other precious herbs, known for warming the kidneys, invigorating yang, and nourishing qi and blood.

Babao Oil

A blend of aromatic and resuscitating herbs, used for heatstroke, dizziness, and abdominal pain; a household essential.

Hugu Zhuifeng Wine

An herbal wine infused with tiger bone (now substituted) and wind-damp dispelling herbs, traditionally used for rheumatic pain and joint stiffness.

Cultural Note

Yekaitai adheres to the ancestral precept 'Though no one sees our crafting, heaven knows our integrity,' upholding the values of selecting premium herbs, following ancient preparation methods, and conducting business with honesty. Its pharmaceutical techniques integrate traditional Chinese processing, emphasizing precise heat and timing, creating a distinctive 'Yekaitai style.' The brand embodies the Chu merchants' spirit of integrity and healing, making it a vital representative of Wuhan's Chinese medicine culture.
