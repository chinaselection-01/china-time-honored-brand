Brand Story

Jinfang, originating in Beijing, derives its name from the imagery of splendid brocade and fragrant blossoms, embodying the city's rich textile and apparel heritage. Although its exact founding year remains unverified, historical clues suggest its development is closely tied to the flourishing handicraft industry of Beijing during the late Qing Dynasty and the early Republic of China (circa 1860-1949). As the political and cultural center, Beijing's textile industry thrived, and Jinfang emerged, renowned for its exquisite embroidery and superior fabrics. While no key figures are officially documented, folklore attributes the brand's founding to a certain Master Li, who skillfully blended Suzhou and Beijing embroidery techniques to create a distinctive style. Jinfang's products were favored by the capital's elite, witnessing the evolution of modern Chinese attire. Culturally, Jinfang is not merely a clothing brand but a carrier of Beijing's local culture, with its patterns conveying auspicious meanings that reflect the aspirations of old Beijingers. Today, despite the challenges of modern commerce, Jinfang's traditional techniques are still preserved by some artisans, making it an important reference for studying Beijing's folk customs and costume history.

Cultural Note

Jinfang's craftsmanship centers on Beijing embroidery, which emphasizes principles of evenness, neatness, fineness, density, uniformity, smoothness, harmony, and luster. The needlework is varied and the colors are rich. Fabrics are typically silk, cotton, and linen, with patterns often featuring dragons, phoenixes, flowers, birds, and symbols of fortune and longevity, reflecting folk wishes for blessings and good luck. The production process is highly manual, involving dozens of steps from design to finished garment, embodying the rigor and artistry of traditional Chinese dressmaking.
