Brand Story

Huang Gou Distillery is located in Yongcheng, Henan, a region with a winemaking history dating back to the Spring and Autumn Period (770-476 BC). Legend has it that Confucius once sought shelter from rain here and tasted the local wine, giving rise to the name 'Huang Gou' (Imperial Ditch). According to the Yongcheng County Annals, during the Ming and Qing dynasties (1368-1912), several family-run breweries, such as the Zhang Family Winery, thrived in the area. They used local high-quality sorghum and wheat, along with mineral-rich well water, and fermented in traditional mud pits to develop a unique flavor. In 1958, the Yongcheng County government consolidated these folk breweries into the state-owned Yongcheng Distillery, later renamed Huang Gou. The enterprise preserves ancient methods while incorporating modern technology, creating a sesame-aroma baijiu that filled a gap in Henan's liquor styles. Huang Gou not only carries local cultural memories but also, through the legend of 'Imperial Huang Gou Wine,' has become a significant symbol of Central Plains liquor culture.

Cultural Note

Huang Gou liquor is crafted using traditional mud-pit fermentation, combined with the techniques of 'high-temperature stacking, high-temperature fermentation, and high-temperature distillation,' resulting in its unique sesame aroma. The brewing process integrates a thousand years of winemaking wisdom from the Central Plains, emphasizing the harmonious unity of 'grain, water, starter, and pit,' reflecting the Chinese baijiu philosophy of 'harmony between heaven and humanity.'
