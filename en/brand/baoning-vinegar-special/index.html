Brand Story

Baoning vinegar originates from Langzhong, Sichuan, historically known as Baoning Prefecture. Its brewing history dates back to the late Ming and early Qing dynasties (around the 17th century). Legend has it that a royal vinegar master named Cui fled to Sichuan to escape turmoil and settled in Baoning. Using local high-quality spring water, wheat bran, rice, and other ingredients, combined with imperial secret recipes, he created a uniquely flavored vinegar. By the early Qing dynasty (18th century), Baoning vinegar was presented as a tribute to the imperial court, gaining widespread fame. During the Republic of China era (early 20th century), it won multiple awards at domestic and international exhibitions, becoming a representative of Sichuan vinegar. After the founding of the People's Republic of China, the Baoning Vinegar Factory (now Sichuan Baoning Vinegar Co., Ltd.) was established, continuing traditional techniques while integrating modern technology. Baoning vinegar is renowned as a 'medicinal vinegar,' requiring dozens of steps including starter-making, fermentation, leaching, and aging, taking over a year to produce. It has a mellow sourness and rich aroma, offering both culinary and health benefits. Beyond being a condiment, Baoning vinegar carries the food culture and migration history of northeastern Sichuan, making it an important cultural symbol of Langzhong Ancient City.

Cultural Note

The brewing technique of Baoning vinegar follows the ancient method of 'summer sun exposure and winter ice removal,' using wheat bran, wheat, and rice as raw materials. It undergoes natural fermentation with multiple microbial strains, and the vinegar mash is aged for an extended period to develop its unique flavor. This technique integrates traditional Chinese food therapy concepts and has been listed as a provincial intangible cultural heritage in Sichuan. Baoning vinegar culture reflects the Sichuan people's philosophy of living in harmony with nature and their pursuit of excellence, making it an integral part of Sichuan cuisine culture.
