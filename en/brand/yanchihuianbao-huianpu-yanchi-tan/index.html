Brand Story

Huianbao, located in Yanchi County, Ningxia, has been a major salt-producing town since ancient times. Historical records show salt officials were stationed here as early as the Han Dynasty. During the Tang Dynasty, it belonged to Yanzhou, and in the Song and Yuan periods, salt production flourished. In the Ming Dynasty, Huianbao was established as a key salt transport hub in the northwest. By the Qianlong era of the Qing Dynasty, salt fields dotted the landscape, and Huianbao thrived as merchants gathered. Generations of local salt workers developed unique techniques of sun-drying and boiling salt. With the rise of sea salt in modern times, Huianbao's salt industry declined, yet traditional craftsmanship and salt culture endure. Today, Huianbao is renowned for its salt-pond lamb, salt lake scenery, and salt industry relics, bearing witness to the region's development and trade.

Cultural Note

Huianbao's salt-making craft dates back to the Han Dynasty, passed down through generations, primarily using sun-drying and boiling methods. Workers utilize natural brine from salt ponds, crystallizing it through solar evaporation or iron-pan boiling. Local salt culture is woven into folk customs, such as salt god worship and salt workers' work chants, reflecting harmony between humans and nature. The Huianbao salt pond ruins serve as vital material evidence for studying ancient salt production.
