Brand Story

Guan, originating from Zhejiang, traces its roots to the mid-Qing Dynasty, around 1820, when the Guan ancestors opened a small workshop near Hangzhou Bay, brewing soy sauce and rice vinegar. Their unique recipes and honest business practices earned them local acclaim. The second generation, led by Guan Wenbing (c. 1850-1920), expanded operations by incorporating Shaoxing rice wine brewing techniques. During the Republican era, the third generation, Guan Shichang (1885-1955), studied modern brewing methods in Shanghai and returned to refine their craft, enhancing quality and expanding distribution to Shanghai and Hangzhou. The family adhered to the ancestral motto: 'Select ingredients with precision, follow strict procedures, and uphold integrity.' Their brewing techniques, harmonizing with the local climate and water, became a cultural emblem of eastern Zhejiang. In 1956, the brand merged into a local food factory, but the Guan family continued to provide technical guidance. Today, Guan stands as a Zhejiang time-honored brand, preserving family memory and regional culture, and remains cherished in the Jiangnan area as a living testament to traditional brewing.

Cultural Note

Guan's brewing techniques integrate the natural conditions of the Jiangnan water towns, using traditional clay vats for fermentation and following seasonal rhythms: 'prepare koji in spring, make sauce in summer, sun-dry in autumn, and brew vinegar in winter.' The process emphasizes 'sun-drying sauce during the hottest days and brewing vinegar during the coldest,' leveraging temperature and humidity variations to promote natural microbial fermentation, preserving the authentic flavors of the ingredients. This craft is not only a family inheritance but also a reflection of Zhejiang's agrarian culture, offering living value for the study of Jiangnan's culinary history.
