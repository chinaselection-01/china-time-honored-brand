Brand Story

Qinghu's roots trace back to the Ming and Qing dynasties in Zhejiang. According to local chronicles, the Qinghu area was a thriving commercial hub due to its convenient water transport, attracting merchants and boosting the food industry. The brand was founded by a craftsman surnamed Chen, who opened a small workshop in Qinghu Town during the Qianlong reign (1736-1795) to brew soy sauce and rice wine. His meticulous selection of ingredients and refined techniques quickly earned local fame. The Chen family passed down and improved the craft through generations. By the Guangxu reign (1875-1908), Qinghu had grown into a family enterprise centered on sauce gardens and breweries, with products sold across neighboring counties. The name 'Qinghu' not only derives from the place but also means 'clear as a lake,' symbolizing purity and superior quality. Though the exact founding year is unrecorded, its clear lineage makes Qinghu a living fossil of Jiangnan's traditional food culture.

Cultural Note

Qinghu's brewing techniques follow ancient methods, adhering to the seasonal rhythm of 'making koji in spring, sun-drying sauce in summer, brewing wine in autumn, and preserving in winter,' reflecting Jiangnan's wisdom of living in harmony with nature. Steps like manual sauce turning and sun-drying in earthenware vats preserve traditional flavors and carry local folk memories. Qinghu products are not just condiments but representatives of the 'fresh, fragrant, and mellow' characteristics of Zhejiang's culinary culture.
