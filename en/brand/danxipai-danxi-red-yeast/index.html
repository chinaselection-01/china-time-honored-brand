Brand Story

Danxi takes its name from Zhu Danxi (1281-1358), a celebrated physician of the Yuan Dynasty, whose hometown, Chian Town in Yiwu, Zhejiang, was historically known as Danxi. Zhu advocated the theory of 'nourishing yin and reducing fire,' which profoundly influenced local dietary culture. To honor his memory, his descendants and villagers brewed yellow rice wine with glutinous rice and medicinal ingredients, creating a unique flavor. During the Ming and Qing dynasties, the brewing industry flourished in Yiwu, and Danxi wineries gradually expanded, producing mellow wines with a lingering finish. In modern times, Danxi has preserved ancient methods while integrating modern technology, becoming a representative of Zhejiang's wine culture. The brand's story carries Zhu Danxi's medical wisdom and the brewing traditions of the Jiangnan water towns, reflecting the philosophy of 'medicine and food share the same origin'—a living heritage of regional culture and historical memory.

Cultural Note

Danxi's brewing technique follows ancient methods, using high-quality glutinous rice with wheat or red yeast as saccharifying agents. The process includes soaking, steaming, fermenting, pressing, and heating the wine, spanning several months. It emphasizes precise temperature and humidity control, embodying the philosophy of 'harmony between heaven and humanity.' Zhu Danxi's medical principle of 'yin-yang balance' is also integrated into brewing, highlighting the wine's mild nature and health benefits when consumed in moderation. This craft is a microcosm of traditional brewing culture in central Zhejiang, holding historical and scientific value.
