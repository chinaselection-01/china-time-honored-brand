Brand Story

Rooted in Shiwan, Foshan, Guangdong, Xinshiwan Meitao traces its origins to a region with a pottery-making history dating back to the Neolithic Age. By the Tang and Song dynasties, Shiwan had become the pottery capital of Lingnan. During the Ming Dynasty (1506-1521), the local ceramic industry flourished, with products exported far and wide. In the late Qing and early Republic periods, renowned Shiwan pottery masters emerged, laying the foundation for modern Shiwan ware. As a contemporary inheritor of this tradition, Xinshiwan Meitao upholds the profound heritage of the Shiwan kilns since the Ming and Qing dynasties. Its key figures include contemporary masters of arts and crafts who skillfully blend traditional sculpting techniques with modern aesthetics, creating works that resonate with both classical charm and contemporary appeal. More than a craft, Xinshiwan Meitao is a vital carrier of Lingnan culture, often depicting themes of rural life and historical tales, reflecting the philosophy and aesthetic sensibilities of the Cantonese people. It serves as a cultural ambassador for the region.

Cultural Note

The craft of Xinshiwan Meitao is inherited from the Shiwan kilns, using local clay and undergoing dozens of processes including clay refining, shaping, glazing, and firing. Its glazes are renowned for rich colors such as jun blue and kingfisher feather glaze. The sculptures emphasize expressive form and vivid portrayal, integrating techniques like round sculpture, relief, and pinching. Masters excel in depicting facial expressions and drapery, showcasing the delicacy and dynamism of Lingnan craftsmanship.
