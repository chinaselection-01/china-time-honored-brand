Brand Story

Jixiang traces its roots to Jiangsu Province, with a history dating back to the Qing Dynasty (1644-1912). According to local chronicles, during the late Qing, Wang Jintang (c. 1840-1910), a craftsman of the Suzhou Imperial Weaving Workshop, founded Jixiang Cloth Shop during the Tongzhi reign (1862-1874), specializing in silk and cotton fabrics. Wang emphasized material selection and craftsmanship, producing fabrics with auspicious patterns that won the hearts of Jiangnan residents. During the Republic era (1912-1949), Jixiang expanded into apparel, becoming renowned for qipao and mandarin jackets with exquisite embroidery and tailoring. After the founding of the People's Republic, Jixiang was integrated into a local state-owned textile factory, preserving its techniques. Today, Jixiang combines handcraft and mechanized production, merging traditional motifs with contemporary design, standing as a representative of Jiangsu's textile culture. Its cultural significance lies in carrying the historical memory of Jiangnan weaving and embodying the profound heritage of Chinese auspicious culture.

Cultural Note

Jixiang's techniques originate from Suzhou's imperial weaving tradition, incorporating intangible cultural heritage crafts such as kesi (silk tapestry) and Suzhou embroidery. The patterns carry symbolic meanings—cloud motifs and bats represent fortune and blessing. The brand adheres to a combination of hand-picked patterns and mechanical weaving to ensure quality, inheriting the water-town culture of Jiangnan and reflecting a pursuit of good fortune and fulfillment.
