Brand Story

Yongchuan Douchi originates from Yongchuan District, Chongqing, with a history dating back to the Ming and Qing dynasties. According to the 'Yongchuan County Annals,' during the Kangxi reign of the Qing Dynasty (1662-1722), local workshops already produced douchi. Nestled in the heart of Bayu, Yongchuan's mild climate and abundant soybean harvests provided ideal raw materials. Legend has it that a renowned Qing-era chef, surnamed Chen, opened a sauce garden in Yongchuan and refined the fermentation process, introducing the 'three-steam, three-sun' method, which gave the douchi its glossy black color and intense aroma. Over time, Yongchuan Douchi became a staple seasoning in Sichuan and Chongqing cuisine, spreading nationwide with the popularity of Sichuan dishes. By the Republican era, it was already sold along the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River. The craft, passed down through generations, embodies the culinary wisdom of the Bayu people and carries deep local nostalgia. Today, Yongchuan Douchi is not just a condiment but a cultural icon of Sichuan cuisine, witnessing the evolution of Chongqing's culinary heritage.

Cultural Note

Yongchuan Douchi is made using traditional solid-state fermentation, with soybeans as the main ingredient. The process involves soaking, steaming, koji-making, and fermenting over several months. The technique emphasizes the 'three-steam, three-sun' method, leveraging natural temperature and humidity to cultivate beneficial microorganisms, resulting in a distinctive flavor. Rich in protein and amino acids, douchi serves both as a seasoning and a nutritious food, reflecting the Bayu people's philosophy of living in harmony with nature.
