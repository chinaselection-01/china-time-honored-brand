Brand Story

Yuloudong, a jewelry brand from Hunan, takes its name from the poetic line 'Jade Tower and Golden Palace' by Tang Dynasty poet Li Bai, symbolizing nobility and eternity. Although its founding year lacks official verification, local chronicles and industry reputation trace its origins to the silver workshops of mid-Qing Dynasty Changsha. At that time, Changsha was a thriving commercial hub in central Hunan, renowned for its exquisite gold and silver craftsmanship. The founding artisans of Yuloudong rose to prominence with masterful engraving and inlay techniques, crafting ornaments for local gentry. During the Republican era, the brand embraced Western Art Deco style, launching a series of jadeite inlaid pieces that were favored by fashionable ladies. After the founding of New China, Yuloudong underwent public-private partnership, yet its craftsmanship heritage endured. In the 21st century, the brand was revitalized, integrating elements of Chu shamanistic totems and Mawangdui patterns into jewelry design, making each piece a wearable cultural emblem. Its cultural significance lies in preserving the lineage of Hunan handmade jewelry while activating tradition with modern aesthetics, serving as a bridge between history and the present.

Cultural Note

Yuloudong upholds traditional gold and silver craftsmanship, including chasing, filigree, and inlay, while incorporating modern 3D modeling and eco-friendly techniques. Its designs often draw inspiration from Hunan's archaeological finds, such as lacquerware patterns from the Mawangdui Han tomb and totems of Miao silver ornaments in western Hunan, reimagined with contemporary aesthetics to make jewelry a vehicle for spreading Huxiang culture.
