Brand Story

Laomiao's origins trace back to the Ming and Qing dynasties, deeply intertwined with the temple fair culture of Shanghai's City God Temple (Chenghuangmiao). Historical records show that during the Yongle reign (1403-1424) of the Ming Dynasty, a temple fair formed around the City God Temple, attracting gold and silver jewelry shops that became a hub for trade and blessings. Laomiao inherits this legacy, its name evoking profound historical depth and auspicious wishes. Although no official founding year is documented, the brand is rooted in the cultural soil of Shanghai's old town, witnessing the city's commercial prosperity in modern times. In the 1990s, Laomiao Gold emerged as a prominent brand alongside other time-honored names like Lao Feng Xiang, quickly gaining market recognition through its commitment to traditional craftsmanship and innovation. Its designs often incorporate Chinese cultural elements such as zodiac signs, the character 'Fu' (blessing), and Ruyi (a symbol of good luck), reflecting people's aspirations for a better life. Laomiao is not just a jeweler but also a purveyor of Shanghai-style culture and traditional Chinese aesthetics.

Cultural Note

Laomiao draws on the culture of Shanghai's City God Temple, blending traditional gold and silver craftsmanship with modern design. Its techniques, inherited from folk artisans in the Jiangnan region, emphasize methods like chasing (zanke) and filigree (huasi), while incorporating contemporary aesthetics. The brand actively promotes cultural heritage through products that convey auspicious themes such as zodiac signs and symbols of fortune, longevity, and happiness, reflecting the rich tapestry of Chinese folk customs.
