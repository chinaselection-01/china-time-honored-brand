Brand Story

Longquan Sword traces its origins to the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, when the legendary swordsmith Ou Yezi forged famous swords like 'Longyuan' and 'Tai'e' at the foot of Qinxi Mountain in Longquan, pioneering the art of Longquan sword making. During the Three Kingdoms period (220-280 AD), Longquan became a major production center for weapons. In the Tang Dynasty (618-907 AD), Longquan swords were listed as tributes to the imperial court. The Song Dynasty (960-1279 AD) saw the flourishing of Longquan's sword industry, establishing the unique 'Longquan Sword' style. During the Ming and Qing dynasties (1368-1911 AD), Longquan swords were sold both domestically and internationally as martial arts equipment and gifts. In modern times, the craft was preserved among the people, and in 1956, the Longquan Sword Factory was established to revive traditional techniques. In 2006, the forging technique of Longquan Sword was inscribed in the first batch of the National Intangible Cultural Heritage list. Longquan Sword is not merely a weapon; it is a symbol of the Chinese martial spirit and craftsmanship, carrying profound historical and cultural significance.

Cultural Note

The forging technique of Longquan Sword involves 28 processes, including forging, scraping, filing, engraving, quenching, and polishing, with the core being 'forging' and 'quenching', emphasizing the balance of hardness and flexibility. The blade is made using locally unique iron sand and bright stone, resulting in exceptional sharpness. The craft is passed down through master-apprentice relationships, emphasizing the unity of mind and hand. Longquan Sword is not only a weapon but also a carrier of Chinese martial arts culture, ritual culture, and aesthetic culture.
