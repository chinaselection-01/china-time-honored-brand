Brand Story

Fanji's la zhi rou jia mo originated in Xi'an, Shaanxi, with a history dating back to the late Qing Dynasty and early Republic of China (around the 1900s). Founder Fan Fengxiang set up a stall at Nan Yuan Men in Xi'an, gaining fame for his unique technique of making la zhi rou (marinated pork). The pork is made from premium cuts, simmered with a blend of spices for hours until tender and succulent, yet not greasy. Fanji's jia mo is known for its 'crispy bread and fragrant meat': the bai ji mo (white sesame flatbread) has a crisp exterior and soft interior, filled with chopped marinated pork and a splash of rich sauce. The craft has been passed down through generations for over a century, making Fanji an iconic snack brand in Xi'an. Culturally, it is more than just food; it carries the memories of Xi'an locals and the city's heritage, reflecting the straightforward and hearty character of Shaanxi people.

Cultural Note

Fanji's la zhi rou technique emphasizes 'fine ingredients, complete spices, and precise heat control.' The pork is simmered for hours with a variety of spices, resulting in tender, flavorful meat. The bai ji mo is made from semi-fermented dough, baked with 'three flips and six turns' to achieve a crispy crust. Passed down for a century, Fanji's craft is a representative of Shaanxi's intangible cultural heritage, embodying the essence of traditional culinary culture.
