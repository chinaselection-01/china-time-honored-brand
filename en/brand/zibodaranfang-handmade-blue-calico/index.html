Brand Story

The origins of Zibo Daranfang can be traced back to the flourishing textile dyeing and printing industry in the Zibo area of Shandong during the Ming and Qing dynasties. Located in central Shandong, Zibo had a long history of sericulture, and by the Ming and Qing periods (1368-1911), Zhoucun had become a major silk distribution center in the north, with numerous dye houses lining the streets. According to local historical records, in the late Qing and early Republic era (approximately 1900-1920), dozens of dye workshops operated in Zhoucun, among which the name 'Daranfang' (literally 'Great Dye House') was the most famous, derived from its large scale and rows of dye vats. Legend has it that Daranfang was jointly founded by local merchants, using traditional plant dyes and hand-printing techniques, with products sold far to Beijing, Tianjin, and the Northeast. Its business philosophy emphasized integrity and quality, gradually building a reputation for 'genuine goods at fair prices, honest with all customers.' Daranfang was not just a production site but also a microcosm of the Lu merchant spirit of 'valuing righteousness over profit.' Its techniques and stories were passed down orally, becoming an important part of Zibo's industrial heritage. However, due to the passage of time, the exact founders and founding year are difficult to ascertain, leaving only ruins and documentary fragments for later generations to remember.

Cultural Note

The core of Zibo Daranfang's craftsmanship lies in the application of plant dyes and hand printing, with its blue calico technique once widely spread across central Shandong. The dye house emphasized natural materials, using indigo from plants like woad and pagoda flower, and patterns often drew from auspicious motifs, reflecting folk aesthetics. Its production model combined family workshops with commercial operations, illustrating the transformation of Shandong's handicraft industry in modern times. Currently, the related techniques have been listed in Zibo's intangible cultural heritage, though the specific level and recognition year require further verification.
