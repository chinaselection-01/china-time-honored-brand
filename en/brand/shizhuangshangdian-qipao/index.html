Brand Story

Shanghai Fashion Store, situated on the bustling East Nanjing Road, traces its origins to early 20th-century tailor shops. In the 1920s, Shanghai was the fashion capital of the Far East, with a thriving garment industry. In 1956, during the wave of public-private partnership, several private clothing stores merged to form Shanghai Fashion Store. As a state-owned department store, it quickly became the city's fashion barometer, witnessing shifts from the Zhongshan suit (Mao suit) to bell-bottoms and international trends. The store gathered master tailors renowned for their handcrafted, custom-made garments. During the planned economy era, it was one of the few places to buy clothing with ration coupons; after reform and opening-up, it pioneered the introduction of international brands. More than a shopping destination, it is a custodian of Shanghai-style dress culture, carrying the memories of generations. Its building, a protected historical landmark, reflects the blend of Chinese and Western aesthetics.

Cultural Note

Shanghai Fashion Store upholds the culture of Shanghai-style dress, merging Chinese and Western tailoring techniques with a focus on handcrafted details. Its custom service preserves the traditional art of measuring and cutting, while embracing modern fashion elements. The store regularly hosts exhibitions on dress culture, promotes traditional garments like the qipao, and participates in intangible cultural heritage protection projects, dedicated to the living heritage of dress culture.
