Brand Story

Founded around 1900 in the late Qing Dynasty by Chef Zhang, a local Chengdu cook, Pan Sun Shi opened its doors near the old South Gate. The name, derived from Du Fu's poem 'Pan Sun Shi Yuan Wu Jian Wei,' reflects the essence of simple home-style cooking. Chef Zhang perfected traditional Sichuan braising techniques, blending a variety of spices to create a unique flavor profile. During the Republican era, Pan Sun Shi became a beloved representative of Chengdu's famous snacks. Its braised dishes, known for their precise heat control and spice ratios, feature a glossy red color and a savory-sweet taste, setting the standard for Sichuan-style marinated cuisine. Culturally, Pan Sun Shi has witnessed a century of Chengdu's culinary evolution, serving as a living fossil of local street culture. The craft has been passed down through generations, preserving traditional flavors and making it a culinary icon of Chengdu.

Cultural Note

Pan Sun Shi's braising technique is rooted in traditional Sichuan methods, emphasizing spice ratios and heat control. The marinade is reused and aged, becoming more flavorful over time. The taste, savory with a hint of sweetness, exemplifies Sichuan cuisine's principle of 'one dish, one style; a hundred dishes, a hundred flavors.' The craft is passed down through master-apprentice relationships, preserving the essence of handmade braising and forming an integral part of Chengdu's food culture.
