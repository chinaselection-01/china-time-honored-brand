Brand Story

Lao Ding Feng's origins trace back to the late Qing Dynasty, around 1911 (the third year of Xuantong), when Wang Ada from Shaoxing, Zhejiang, founded it in Harbin. At that time, Harbin was a bustling hub on the Chinese Eastern Railway, attracting merchants from all over. Wang Ada, with his exquisite pastry skills, opened a shop with a front store and back factory, naming it 'Lao Ding Feng', symbolizing 'prosperity and abundance'. His products blended northern and southern flavors, especially famous for mooncakes and oil tea flour, quickly winning the hearts of locals. During the Republic of China era, despite wars and turmoil, Lao Ding Feng adhered to traditional handmade methods, becoming an unforgettable taste for Harbin residents. After the founding of New China, the brand underwent public-private partnership, preserving its techniques. Today, Lao Ding Feng is listed as a Heilongjiang provincial intangible cultural heritage, with its pastry-making skills carrying the city's memories and serving as a tasteful testament to the fusion of Chinese and Western cultures.

Cultural Note

Lao Ding Feng's pastry-making techniques integrate Beijing, Suzhou, and Russian styles, emphasizing hand-kneaded dough, hand-wrapped fillings, and careful baking with precise heat and ingredients. Its traditional mooncake molds are intricately carved, reflecting folk art. As a Harbin time-honored brand, it witnesses the city's immigrant culture and culinary fusion, serving as an important medium for studying modern Northeast Chinese folk customs.
