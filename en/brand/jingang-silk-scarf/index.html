Brand Story

Jingang, a textile brand from Liaoning, traces its roots to the late Qing Dynasty and early Republic of China (around 1900). At that time, the textile industry in Liaoning was emerging, and the founder of Jingang [to be verified] established a handcraft workshop in Shenyang, renowned for traditional weaving techniques. Over a century, Jingang has integrated Guandong culture with modern craftsmanship, becoming a representative of Northeast China's textile industry. The brand name symbolizes 'a harbor of splendor,' reflecting the brilliance of textile arts and the inclusiveness of a port. During the industrial revolution, Jingang introduced advanced equipment while preserving handcrafted essence, with products sold both domestically and internationally. Its cultural significance lies in inheriting Manchu brocade techniques and Han embroidery, witnessing the industrial history of Liaoning, and serving as a living memory of the old industrial base in the Northeast.

Cultural Note

Jingang's craftsmanship blends traditional Manchu brocade with Han embroidery, using natural dyes and hand-operated jacquard weaving. Techniques include 'tongjing duanwei' (warp-through, weft-cut) and 'panjin xiu' (coiled gold thread embroidery), requiring dozens of steps and months of work. The brand adheres to 'using cloth as paper, thread as ink,' passing down century-old craftsmanship and reflecting the regional culture of Liaoning.
