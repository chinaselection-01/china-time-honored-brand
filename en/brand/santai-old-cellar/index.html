Brand Story

Santai Wine traces its roots to the Qianlong era of the Qing Dynasty (1736-1795), when Santai Town in Jimsar County, Xinjiang, flourished as a key hub on the ancient Silk Road. The bustling trade brought prosperity, and local brewing industry emerged. By the Guangxu period (1875-1908), several distilleries operated in the area, using Tianshan snowmelt water, sorghum, and wheat to produce liquor through traditional solid-state fermentation. The resulting spirit was clear, crisp, and refreshing, winning favor among traveling merchants. During the Republic era, Santai's brewing industry expanded, establishing 'Santai Shaojiu' as a renowned local brand. After the founding of New China, the Santai Distillery was formally established in 1958, consolidating local resources while preserving traditional methods and gradually incorporating modern technology. Today, Santai Wine stands as a living testament to Xinjiang's brewing history, witnessing the region's multicultural exchanges and Silk Road prosperity. Its techniques, passed down through generations, reflect the unwavering pursuit of quality by the people of Xinjiang.

Cultural Note

The traditional brewing techniques of Santai Wine encompass starter-making, fermentation, distillation, and aging, utilizing mud-pit solid-state fermentation and Tianshan snowmelt water. The precise ingredient ratios and process parameters are orally transmitted by master craftsmen. The resulting spirit embodies both the boldness and finesse characteristic of northwestern Chinese Baijiu, showcasing Xinjiang's regional identity and artisan spirit.
