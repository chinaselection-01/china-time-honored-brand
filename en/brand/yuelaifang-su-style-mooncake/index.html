Brand Story

Yuelai Fang traces its origins to Shanghai in the late Qing Dynasty. During the Guangxu reign (c. 1875-1908), as Shanghai prospered after opening as a port, the food trade flourished. The brand began as a shop specializing in Suzhou-style tea snacks, known for its fine craftsmanship and premium ingredients. During the Republic era (1912-1949), Yuelai Fang grew steadily, winning the hearts of Shanghai residents with its pastries and mooncakes, becoming a notable name in the local food industry. The name 'Yuelai Fang' conveys the aspiration to 'attract customers from near and far, leaving a fragrant legacy for generations,' reflecting the founders' pursuit of quality and reputation. Though the exact founding year is not officially documented, Yuelai Fang's history is closely intertwined with Shanghai's modern commercial development, making it a valuable example of Haipai food culture.

Cultural Note

Yuelai Fang adheres to traditional Suzhou-style pastry-making techniques, insisting on hand-made flaky crusts and charcoal baking, while prioritizing fresh ingredients and authentic flavors. Its products blend Jiangnan culinary culture, reflecting Shanghai residents' pursuit of refined living. The brand innovates within tradition, merging classic tastes with modern consumer needs, and stands as a representative of Haipai food culture.
