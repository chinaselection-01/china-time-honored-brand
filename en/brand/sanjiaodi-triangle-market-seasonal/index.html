Brand Story

Sanjiaodi, located in Shanghai's old city, derives its name from the triangular plot formed by the intersection of three roads during the Ming and Qing dynasties. After Shanghai's city walls were built in the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644), a market gradually emerged here. By the mid-Qing Dynasty (1644-1912), Sanjiaodi had become a key trading site for fresh produce and aquatic products, especially known for its early-morning wholesale market. In the early 20th century, the expansion of foreign concessions and municipal development spurred commercial growth, turning Sanjiaodi into a vibrant neighborhood centered around its food market. In the 1920s, the Sanjiaodi Market was formally established, becoming one of Shanghai's largest indoor markets. Though the market's function changed after the 1950s due to urban renewal, the name persists, carrying the collective memories of generations. Without a single founder, Sanjiaodi's cultural significance lies in its role as a public space woven into Shanghai's urban history.

Cultural Note

As a traditional market in the old city, Sanjiaodi embodies the 'shijing' (marketplace) spirit of Shanghai's modern commercial culture. Its scenes of dawn trading, bargaining, and neighborly familiarity fostered a unique community bond. The market was not merely a place for commerce but also a space for information exchange and folk customs. Sanjiaodi witnessed Shanghai's evolution from a traditional county seat to a modern city, and its name and memories have become part of the city's intangible cultural heritage, reflecting the inclusive and pragmatic nature of Shanghai-style culture.
