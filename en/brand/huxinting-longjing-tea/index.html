Brand Story

Huxinting Teahouse, located in Shanghai's Yu Garden, was first built in 1784 (the 49th year of Emperor Qianlong's reign) by local gentry as a pavilion for rest. In 1855, it was converted into a teahouse, becoming one of the earliest in Shanghai. During the late Qing and early Republic periods, it became a gathering place for literati and artists, including renowned Shanghai School painters Ren Bonian and Wu Changshuo, who often came to sip tea and discuss art, leaving behind many anecdotes. Over the centuries, the teahouse has witnessed Shanghai's transformation from a Jiangnan water town to a global metropolis. Its architecture blends traditional Jiangnan garden style with Western elements, epitomizing the eclectic Haipai culture. Today, Huxinting is not only a destination for tea lovers but also a vital repository of Shanghai's collective memory, carrying the nostalgia and sentiments of generations.

Cultural Note

The tea ceremony at Huxinting emphasizes 'tranquility, harmony, and elegance,' focusing on the unity of environment and mood. The teahouse preserves the traditional covered-bowl brewing technique, paired with Jiangnan-style tea snacks, reflecting the refinement and inclusiveness of Haipai tea culture. Its architecture complements the Yu Garden landscape, serving as a living heritage of Jiangnan garden tea culture.
