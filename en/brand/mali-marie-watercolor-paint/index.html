Brand Story

Marie's originated in early 20th-century Shanghai. In 1919, amid the May Fourth Movement's call for domestic goods, educator and painter Zhang Yuguang, together with partner Hong Jitang, founded Marie's Craft Society to produce Chinese-made art pigments and break foreign dominance. Zhang, who had served as principal of Shanghai Art College, was driven by the high cost and unstable supply of imported paints. The name 'Marie's' (Mali) symbolized 'immediate success and benefiting the nation and its people.' Initially producing watercolors and oil paints, the brand quickly gained recognition for its quality and fair prices. By the 1920s, its products reached Southeast Asia, becoming a favorite among overseas Chinese artists. In the 1930s, the company was restructured into a limited company with advanced equipment. During the War of Resistance, it relocated to Chongqing, sustaining production to support art education. After 1949, it continued as a public-private enterprise. Marie's not only supplied materials but also contributed to China's modern art education system; masters like Xu Beihong and Liu Haisu used its pigments.

Cultural Note

Marie's Craft Society was China's first national pigment enterprise, and its history mirrors the development of the Chinese art pigment industry. The brand preserves traditional pigment techniques while integrating modern chemistry to develop products suited to Chinese painting, oil painting, and other genres. Its craftsmanship emphasizes fine grinding of pigments and precise binder ratios to ensure stability. Marie's pigments have witnessed the fusion of Chinese and Western art, providing the material foundation for modern Chinese art education.
