Brand Story

Weiyizhai is a long-established food brand in Shandong, whose name means 'the only authentic one.' According to local chronicles and folk records, its origins can be traced back to the mid-Qing Dynasty (around 1800 AD), when Linyi, Shandong, was a bustling commercial hub with numerous sauce gardens and pastry shops. Founded by a local merchant surnamed Wang, it started as a small workshop specializing in soy sauce, vinegar, and pickled vegetables. Thanks to the selection of fine ingredients and meticulous craftsmanship, its products quickly gained a reputation and became famous throughout the region. By the late Qing Dynasty (around 1900), Weiyizhai had expanded into a front-shop-back-factory operation, with products sold to neighboring counties. The second-generation inheritor, Wang Shichang, improved the brewing process by introducing the 'three-summer sun-drying' method, resulting in soy sauce with a bright red color and rich aroma. Culturally, Weiyizhai has witnessed the evolution of dietary culture in southern Shandong, and its traditional brewing techniques embody ancient Chinese fermentation wisdom. However, due to the loss of historical documents, the exact founding year and early inheritance details remain to be verified. Having endured wars and institutional changes in modern times, the brand is now revived by a local enterprise to continue its traditional flavors.

Cultural Note

Weiyizhai's brewing techniques follow ancient methods, emphasizing 'making koji in spring, sun-drying sauce in summer, and extracting oil in autumn,' utilizing natural temperature and humidity for fermentation over a period of up to one year. The sauce vats are arranged in neat rows and require manual stirring at regular intervals, reflecting the traditional philosophy of harmony between heaven and humanity. This craft is not merely food processing but a living inheritance of intangible cultural heritage, carrying the taste memories of the people of southern Shandong.
