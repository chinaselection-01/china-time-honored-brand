Brand Story

Heji originated in Henan, with its exact founding year unverified, but local historical records trace its predecessor to a folk eatery in the mid-to-late Qing Dynasty (around 1800 AD). At that time, Henan, located in the Central Plains, was rich in produce and had a deep culinary culture. The founder (name to be confirmed) started by selling local snacks and gradually developed the establishment into a proper restaurant serving authentic Yu cuisine. Heji adheres to the cooking philosophy of 'fine ingredient selection and precise heat control,' blending the characteristics of Yu cuisine—'balanced flavors and moderate tastes'—which won the hearts of local people. Through generations of inheritance, Heji gained considerable fame during the Republic of China period (1912-1949), becoming one of Henan's representative dining establishments. Its cultural significance lies in preserving traditional Yu cooking techniques and passing down the historical memories and folk customs of the Central Plains through food, making it an important carrier of Henan's culinary culture.

Cultural Note

Heji's culinary skills emphasize 'enhancing freshness with broth and determining flavor with heat.' Its broth-making and braising techniques, inherited from Yu cuisine tradition, embody the Central Plains philosophy of 'harmony and balance.' Heji insists on using local ingredients, such as Yellow River carp and wheat from northern Henan, to ensure authentic flavors. Its dishes not only satisfy appetites but also carry the nostalgia and hospitality of Henan people.
