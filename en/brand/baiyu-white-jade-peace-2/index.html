Brand Story

Baiyu's origins trace back to the vibrant era of modern Shanghai, when the city's opening as a port sparked a cultural renaissance. Amidst this fusion of East and West, the jewelry trade flourished, and Baiyu emerged from this fertile ground. Though the exact founding year remains unrecorded, historical industry records suggest its predecessor was a jewelry workshop active during the late Qing Dynasty and early Republic of China. There, skilled artisans combined traditional jade carving techniques with Western setting methods, forging a distinctive style. Centering on white jade, the brand prioritizes material quality and design, earning the favor of Shanghai's elite. Culturally, Baiyu embodies the inclusive spirit of Haipai culture, preserving the warmth and elegance of the East while embracing contemporary aesthetics. While no specific founder is documented, generations of craftsmen have passed down their skills, ensuring the brand's continuity. Its works often feature motifs of flowers, birds, and landscapes, symbolizing auspiciousness, and have become a microcosm of Shanghai's jewelry heritage.

Cultural Note

Baiyu's craftsmanship inherits the Haipai jade carving tradition, emphasizing material selection and carving skill, guided by the principle of 'adapting the art to the material.' Its techniques blend the delicacy of Suzhou-style carving with Shanghai's innovative spirit, employing methods such as relief and openwork carving to create flowing lines and vivid forms. The brand also incorporates Western jewelry design concepts, combining white jade with diamonds and colored gemstones to achieve a unique East-meets-West style. Culturally, white jade symbolizes the virtues of a gentleman, and through its creations, Baiyu conveys Eastern aesthetics while catering to modern consumer tastes.
