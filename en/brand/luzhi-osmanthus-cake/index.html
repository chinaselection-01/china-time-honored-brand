Brand Story

Luzhi Ancient Town, located in Suzhou, Jiangsu, boasts a history dating back to the Spring and Autumn Period (514 BC). Its abundant water-town resources nurtured a distinctive local cuisine. During the Ming and Qing dynasties (1368-1912), Luzhi flourished as a commercial hub in Jiangnan, where pastry and pickled vegetable making techniques developed. According to local chronicles, by the late Qing and early Republic era (around 1900), several pastry shops in Luzhi were famed for handmade osmanthus cakes and Dingsheng cakes. These treats, made from local glutinous rice and osmanthus, undergo steaming, pounding, and molding to achieve a soft, chewy texture with a delicate sweetness, perfect for festivals and hospitality. Though Luzhi brand lacks a precise founding year, its craft, passed down through generations, reflects the culinary wisdom of the Jiangnan water towns. Culturally, Luzhi pastries are intertwined with local customs, such as New Year rice cakes and Dragon Boat Festival zongzi, symbolizing blessings and reunion. Today, Luzhi continues this tradition, becoming an integral part of Suzhou's gastronomic heritage.

Cultural Note

Luzhi's food craftsmanship is predominantly manual, emphasizing ingredient selection and seasonality—osmanthus cakes in spring, rice cakes in winter. The process involves soaking, grinding, steaming, and shaping, preserving the authentic flavors of Jiangnan pastries. The skills are typically passed down within families or from master to apprentice, exemplifying the continuity of folk handicraft.
