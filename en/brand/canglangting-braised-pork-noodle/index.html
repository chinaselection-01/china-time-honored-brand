Brand Story

The Canglangting brand originated in Shanghai, its name inspired by the classical Suzhou garden, Canglangting (the Blue Wave Pavilion), symbolizing elegance and tranquility. In the early 20th century, as the catering industry flourished in Shanghai's concessions, a wave of Suzhou-style restaurants emerged. Canglangting was founded during the Republic of China era (1912-1949), with the exact year yet to be verified. The founder, said to be a chef from Suzhou, brought his expertise in Suzhou-style noodles and pastries to Shanghai, winning over diners with delicate soup noodles and rice cakes. Through the period of public-private partnership and the reform and opening-up, the brand has adhered to traditional handmade techniques, becoming a representative of Suzhou-style snacks in Shanghai. Its cultural significance lies in integrating Suzhou garden aesthetics into dining, embodying the refined lifestyle of Jiangnan literati, and serving as a living fossil of Suzhou culinary traditions within Shanghai's eclectic food culture.

Cultural Note

Canglangting upholds traditional Suzhou noodle-making techniques, including hand-rolled noodles, slow-simmered broth, and carefully prepared toppings. The broth is clear yet flavorful, and the noodles are smooth and springy. Dim sum follows the seasons, such as green rice balls in spring and fresh pork mooncakes in autumn. The brand emphasizes the natural flavors of ingredients, preserving the light and elegant style of Jiangnan cuisine, and is an important inheritor of Suzhou culinary skills in Shanghai.
