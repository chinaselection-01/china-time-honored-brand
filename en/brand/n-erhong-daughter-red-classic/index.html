Brand Story

Nü'er Hong, or 'Daughter's Red Wine,' traces its origins to 304 AD during the Western Jin dynasty, as recorded in Ji Han's 'Nanfang Caomu Zhuang' (Plants of the South). By 1131 AD, during the Southern Song dynasty, Shaoxing's brewing industry flourished, and Nü'er Hong became an integral part of local wedding traditions. Legend has it that a tailor in Shaoxing, longing for a child, buried a jar of rice wine under an osmanthus tree upon his daughter's birth. When she married years later, he unearthed the wine for the wedding feast. Its rich aroma and exquisite taste won universal praise, and the wine was aptly named 'Nü'er Hong.' This custom has been passed down for over a millennium in the Jiangnan region, symbolizing family joy and blessings. More than just a wine, Nü'er Hong embodies the continuity of life and familial bonds in Jiangnan culture. Its brewing techniques, handed down through generations, reflect the craftsmanship and cultural heritage of a Chinese time-honored brand.

Cultural Note

The brewing of Nü'er Hong follows ancient methods, using high-quality glutinous rice and water from Jianhu Lake. The process involves soaking, steaming, fermenting, pressing, and pasteurizing, followed by years of aging. The resulting wine is clear with an orange hue, sweet and mellow in taste, and rich in both nutrition and flavor. In Jiangnan culture, Nü'er Hong represents family completeness and happiness, serving as a core element in wedding ceremonies and reflecting the profound heritage of traditional Chinese wine culture.
