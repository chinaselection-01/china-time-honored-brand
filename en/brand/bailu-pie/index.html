Brand Story

Bailu, a brand from Xiamen, Fujian, takes its name from the city's iconic egret, symbolizing good fortune and coastal elegance. Local records indicate that pastry-making in Xiamen dates back to the Ming and Qing dynasties, with numerous pastry shops flourishing. Although the exact founding year of Bailu is not officially documented, it is believed to have originated from a pastry workshop established by local artisans during the late Qing and early Republic periods, known for handmade traditional treats. By the mid-20th century, as Xiamen's food industry consolidated, the Bailu brand emerged, integrating Minnan pastry techniques with maritime cultural elements. The brand insists on using premium local ingredients such as glutinous rice, peanuts, and seaweed to craft specialties like pie (馅饼) and gongtang (贡糖). More than just food, Bailu carries the collective memory of Xiamen, evoking nostalgia among overseas Chinese. Its products once traveled with emigrants to Southeast Asia, serving as a link between homeland and abroad. Today, Bailu continues to honor ancient methods while innovating flavors, aiming to bring the taste of Minnan to a broader audience.

Cultural Note

Bailu preserves the traditional pastry-making techniques of Minnan, emphasizing handcrafted processes such as repeatedly folding and rolling the flaky crust of pies and mastering the heat and pulling skills for gongtang. The brand prioritizes authentic ingredients, using local peanuts and glutinous rice, reflecting the fusion of maritime and agricultural cultures. Its products are often featured in festivals and rituals, making them an integral part of Minnan folk customs.
