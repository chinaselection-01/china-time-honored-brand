Brand Story

Yichang, originating in Hunan, takes its name from the auspicious phrase 'Yizhao Fengchang,' symbolizing abundance and prosperity. While the exact founding year is not officially confirmed, local chronicles and oral histories trace its roots to the mid-to-late Qing Dynasty (c. 1800-1850), when a pastry master surnamed Zhou from Changsha Prefecture established a small shop. Known for his flaky pastries, Zhou's signature 'Su Pi Yue Bing' (crisp-skinned mooncakes) and 'Gui Hua Gao' (osmanthus cakes) won the hearts of locals. By the early 20th century, his descendants introduced northern pastry techniques, blending flavors from north and south. During the War of Resistance (1937-1945), Yichang supplied dried rations to soldiers, reflecting its patriotic spirit. After 1949, the brand underwent public-private partnership, preserving its craft. Today, Yichang remains committed to handmade methods, with each pastry serving as a vessel of Hunan's cultural memory.

Cultural Note

Yichang's pastry-making integrates techniques from both northern and southern China, emphasizing premium ingredients, hand-shaping, and charcoal baking. The flaky crust requires a precise balance of water-oil dough and oil pastry, folded and rolled repeatedly to create dozens of layers. Beyond food, these pastries are integral to Hunan's festive and wedding customs, offering valuable insight into the region's culinary heritage.
