Brand Story

Originating in Jinhua, Zhejiang, the Xuefang Jiang brand dates back to the mid-Qing Dynasty (around 1800 AD), founded by the Jiang family. The founder, Jiang Xuefang (c. 1780-1850), refined the traditional Jinhua ham curing and fermentation methods, selecting premium local pork hind legs and using well salt. Through a meticulous process of winter curing, spring air-drying, and summer storage, the ham develops a thin skin, fine bones, bright red flesh, and a savory-sweet flavor with a lingering finish. The Jiang family operated the business for generations, and by the late Qing and early Republic period (around 1900), Xuefang Jiang ham had become famous across Jiangsu and Zhejiang, sold widely in Shanghai and Hangzhou, and prized as a delicacy at banquets. Culturally, Xuefang Jiang is not only an outstanding representative of Jinhua ham but also embodies the refinement and craftsmanship of Jiangnan cuisine. Its techniques have been passed down to this day, making it a vital symbol of local culinary memory.

Cultural Note

The production of Xuefang Jiang ham involves dozens of steps, including selecting, trimming, curing, washing, sun-drying, shaping, and fermenting, spanning about ten months. The salting process is meticulous, adjusted according to temperature and humidity, and the fermentation period lasts several months, resulting in a rich, mellow flavor. The craft emphasizes the 'three-needle' quality test, where bamboo skewers are inserted into different parts of the ham to sniff the aroma, ensuring quality. This technique is a living heritage of Jinhua ham culture, showcasing the wisdom of traditional food processing.
