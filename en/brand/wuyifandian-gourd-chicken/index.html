Brand Story

Wuyi Restaurant is situated near the Bell Tower in Xi'an. Its origins trace back to the 1950s when, in 1954, the Xi'an Food Company consolidated several private eateries to establish the 'Wuyi Canteen' at the northeast corner of the Bell Tower, later renamed Wuyi Restaurant. The name 'Wuyi' (May Day) symbolizes the spirit of the working class, reflecting the era's ethos. From its inception, the restaurant gathered renowned Shaanxi chefs, such as Jin Xuanmin, who specialized in Huluji (gourd chicken), and they carried forward and innovated Shaanxi culinary techniques. The restaurant is celebrated for its authentic Shaanxi dishes and Xi'an-style snacks, including Huluji, Wenban Yaosi (warm mixed shredded pork kidney), and Paopao Yougao (bubble oil cake), all beloved by locals and tourists alike. Over the decades, Wuyi Restaurant has not only been a place to savor delicious food but also a witness to Xi'an's urban transformation, carrying the taste memories of generations and becoming a significant symbol of Xi'an's food culture.

Signature Products

Huluji (Gourd Chicken)

A classic Shaanxi dish, whole chicken deep-fried to resemble a gourd, with crispy skin and tender meat that easily separates from the bone. Approximately 58-88 RMB per serving.

Wenban Yaosi (Warm Mixed Shredded Pork Kidney)

Pork kidney sliced into thin strips, prepared with the warm mixing technique, resulting in a crisp-tender texture with a sour, spicy, and savory flavor, free of any gamey taste. Approximately 38-58 RMB per serving.

Paopao Yougao (Bubble Oil Cake)

A traditional Xi'an snack with a crispy exterior and sweet, soft filling that melts in the mouth, shaped like bubbles. Approximately 3-5 RMB each.

Cultural Note

Wuyi Restaurant's culinary heritage centers on Shaanxi cuisine, emphasizing precise heat control and knife skills. For instance, Huluji requires multiple steps of boiling, steaming, and deep-frying. The 'warm mixing' technique is unique to Shaanxi, involving hot ingredients served cold to preserve natural flavors. The restaurant insists on traditional handmade preparation, and some dishes require advance reservation. These skills are passed down through master-apprentice mentorship, reflecting the profound cultural depth of Shaanxi cuisine.
