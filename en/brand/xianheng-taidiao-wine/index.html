Brand Story

Founded in 1894 during the Qing Dynasty by the Zhou clan at Duchangfangkou, Shaoxing, Xianheng Tavern began as a modest wine shop serving yellow wine and snacks like fennel beans. Its name, derived from the I Ching's Kun hexagram, means 'all things prosper.' After closing in the late Qing due to poor management, it was rebuilt in 1981 near the original site to commemorate the centenary of Lu Xun's birth. Lu Xun's short story 'Kong Yiji' immortalized the tavern as a literary symbol of old Jiangnan wine houses, with its L-shaped counter, warm wine, and fennel beans becoming cultural icons. Today, Xianheng is not just a tavern but a living embodiment of Shaoxing yellow wine culture, continuing the warm, everyday hospitality of a 'small shop with aged wine.'

“Small shop, aged wine; Xianheng, a century of tradition.”

Cultural Note

Xianheng Tavern preserves the traditional front-shop-back-workshop layout. Its yellow wine is brewed following ancient methods using Jianhu Lake water, glutinous rice, and wheat starter, undergoing soaking, steaming, fermentation, and pressing—brewed in winter and matured by spring. The wine culture here is intertwined with Lu Xun's literature, making it a symbol of Shaoxing folk customs and culinary memory.
