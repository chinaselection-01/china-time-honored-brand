Brand Story

Yeshi Crafts originated in Hunan, with a history tracing back to the mid-Qing Dynasty (around 1800 AD), when the Yeshi ancestors were renowned for woodcarving and bamboo carving in central Hunan. According to family records, the Yeshi family has been artisans for generations, excelling in incorporating traditional patterns into everyday objects, forming a unique style. In the late Qing and early Republic period (around 1900), Yeshi Qiming, a descendant, opened a workshop in Changsha, blending Xiang embroidery with carving techniques to create crafts that were both practical and ornamental, favored by local literati. During the War of Resistance, the craft was nearly lost, but the family persevered, passing down techniques orally. After the founding of New China, Yeshi Crafts was revived and gradually entered the market. Today, Yeshi Crafts remains committed to handmade production, with each piece carrying the memory of Huxiang culture and exemplifying the spirit of craftsmanship.

Cultural Note

Yeshi Crafts integrates Hunan's woodcarving, bamboo carving, and Xiang embroidery, emphasizing traditional motifs such as dragons, phoenixes, flowers, and birds, reflecting the simplicity and vitality of Hunan folk art. The production process strictly follows ancient methods, requiring dozens of steps from material selection to completion, each meticulously crafted. Yeshi Crafts is not just a craft but a vital carrier of Huxiang culture, reflecting the local people's aesthetic and life philosophy.
