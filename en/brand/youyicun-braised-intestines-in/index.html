Brand Story

Youyicun is a well-known time-honored restaurant brand in Shandong, with a history dating back to the late Qing Dynasty (around the 19th century). According to legend, the founder first opened a small eatery in Jinan Prefecture, serving home-style Lu cuisine and handmade pastries. Thanks to its authentic flavors and affordable prices, it gradually won the favor of local residents. During the Republic of China era (1912-1949), Youyicun expanded with branches in Jinan, Qingdao, and other cities, becoming a leading Lu cuisine restaurant. Its signature dishes, such as Jiuzhuan Dachang (braised pork intestines in soy sauce) and Tangcu Liyu (sweet and sour carp), are known for their precise cooking techniques and seasoning, embodying the savory and mellow characteristics of Lu cuisine. After the public-private partnership in 1956, Youyicun became state-owned, preserving traditional skills and training a number of renowned Lu cuisine chefs. Following the reform and opening-up, the brand regained vitality, but due to market pressures, some old branches closed, leaving only a few outlets today. Culturally, Youyicun carries the memory of Shandong's culinary traditions, and its pastry-making techniques (such as Gaozhuang Mantou, a type of steamed bun) have been listed as a municipal intangible cultural heritage. Although the official founding year has not been verified, historical records indicate it has operated for at least a century.

Cultural Note

Youyicun adheres to traditional Lu cuisine techniques, such as bao (quick-frying), pa (braising), shao (stewing), and zha (deep-frying), with a focus on stock preparation and heat control. Its pastries are made using old dough fermentation, a meticulous process. The brand inherits the dietary customs of Jinan Prefecture and serves as an important sample for studying the development of Lu cuisine.
