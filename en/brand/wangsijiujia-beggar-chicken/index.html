Brand Story

Wang Si Restaurant, located in Changshu, Jiangsu, traces its origins to the Qing Dynasty's Guangxu era (circa 1875-1908). Founded by the Wang family, it began as a modest countryside eatery and gradually gained renown for its distinctive cooking techniques. The founder, Wang Si (full name unknown), skillfully utilized local ingredients, creating signature dishes such as Beggar's Chicken and brewing his own osmanthus wine, which endeared him to the community. During the Republic era (1912-1949), the restaurant's reputation flourished, attracting literati and scholars who frequented the establishment, leaving behind many anecdotes. Through wars and societal changes, the restaurant has steadfastly adhered to traditional methods, becoming a living testament to Jiangnan's culinary culture. Its Beggar's Chicken, wrapped in lotus leaves and sealed with yellow mud before roasting, yields tender, fall-off-the-bone meat. The osmanthus wine, brewed with golden osmanthus flowers, is clear, sweet, and mellow, embodying the region's philosophy of 'eating by the seasons.' Wang Si Restaurant is not merely a dining venue but a cultural symbol, witnessing the evolution of local life in the Jiangnan water towns.

Cultural Note

Wang Si Restaurant's craft centers on 'earthen stove, lotus leaves, and yellow mud.' The preparation of Beggar's Chicken involves over a dozen steps, including marinating, wrapping, and baking, with heat control relying on experience. The osmanthus wine is made through traditional fermentation, with osmanthus flowers harvested in autumn and fermented with glutinous rice over several months. The cuisine integrates the aquatic bounty of Changshu, emphasizing the natural flavors of ingredients, reflecting the Jiangnan wisdom of 'eating by the seasons.'
