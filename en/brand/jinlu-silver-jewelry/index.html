Brand Story

The Jinlu brand originates from Inner Mongolia, with its roots in the gold and silver ornament-making traditions of nomadic herding cultures. Historical records show that as early as the Yuan Dynasty (1271-1368), Mongolian artisans were skilled in crafting horse fittings and jewelry from gold and silver, a craft passed down through generations. The brand name 'Jinlu' (Golden Deer) draws from the deer, a sacred animal of the grasslands, symbolizing auspiciousness and vitality. Although the exact founding year is not officially documented, the brand inherits the fine metalwork techniques of northern ethnic groups, blending Mongolian patterns with Central Plains craftsmanship. During the Qing Dynasty (1636-1912), Guihua (now Hohhot) became a major hub for jewelry trade in the north, and Jinlu may have been associated with local artisan families. The brand's cultural significance lies in bridging grassland civilization and Central Plains commerce, with its works often featuring deer antlers, cloud motifs, and hada elements, reflecting the aesthetics and beliefs of nomadic peoples. However, specific details about its founders and early development remain scarce and require further research.

Cultural Note

Jinlu relies on Mongolian gold and silver fine metalwork, a craft that matured during the Yuan Dynasty, characterized by hammering, chasing, and inlaying, with patterns drawn from nature and religion. The brand emphasizes the inheritance of traditional craftsmanship, though its specific intangible cultural heritage status is yet to be confirmed. Its cultural value lies in preserving the artistic symbols of nomadic peoples and promoting the contemporary transformation of ethnic handicrafts.
