Brand Story

The Sixth Grain and Oil Store, formally known as Shanghai No.6 Grain and Oil Food Store, traces its roots back to the 1950s. In 1955, following the socialist transformation of private grain and oil businesses, state-owned grain and oil stores were established across Shanghai's districts, and the Sixth Grain and Oil Store emerged as a vital outlet ensuring the supply of daily necessities. During the planned economy era, it distributed rationed grain and oil to local residents, becoming an indispensable part of life for generations of Shanghainese. After the reform and opening-up, as the grain and oil market liberalized, the store transitioned from a planned to a market economy, yet it remained rooted in the community, providing grain, oil, and various food products. The 'Sixth' in its name reflects the regionally organized state-owned commercial system of the time. Although the exact founding year is no longer verifiable, as a witness to Shanghai's commercial evolution, the Sixth Grain and Oil Store carries the city's memory and exemplifies the resilience and warmth of a state-owned old brand amid the currents of change.

Cultural Note

As a typical representative of state-owned grain and oil stores, the Sixth Grain and Oil Store's business model and service philosophy embody the planned economy's principle of 'guaranteeing supply and serving people's livelihoods.' In times of scarcity, it strictly implemented national policies and ensured fair distribution, serving as a core node of community life. Its cultural significance lies in being not just a commercial outlet but a cornerstone of social stability, carrying collective memory and community identity. Today, it continues the time-honored tradition of integrity with affordable prices and reliable quality.
