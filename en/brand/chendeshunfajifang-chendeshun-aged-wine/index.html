Brand Story

Chen Deshun Faji Fang, located in Zhejiang Province, traces its origins to the mid-Qing Dynasty (around 1800, during the Jiaqing era), according to local chronicles and family genealogies. Founder Chen Deshun, a descendant of a Shaoxing brewing family, relocated to another part of Zhejiang and established his winery, naming it 'Faji' to signify prosperity. The Chen family has upheld traditional Huangjiu brewing methods for generations, using glutinous rice, wheat qu, and Jianhu water. The process involves soaking, steaming, fermenting, pressing, and pasteurizing, resulting in a mellow, sweet, and lingering flavor. The winery reached its zenith in the late Qing and early Republic era (1900-1920), with products sold in Shanghai and Hangzhou, beloved by literati. Beyond brewing, it represents Jiangnan wine culture, intertwined with local customs and festivals.

Cultural Note

Chen Deshun Faji Fang adheres to ancient brewing methods, using traditional clay jars for aging and following seasonal rhythms—brewing in winter and maturing in spring. Its technique emphasizes a 'three mashes, four waters' ratio, with wheat qu crafted by experience, showcasing Jiangnan brewing wisdom. The winery integrates with local customs, holding a ritual to honor the wine god during the Winter Solstice, preserving wine heritage.
