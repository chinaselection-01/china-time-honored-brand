Brand Story

Guilin, historically part of Baiyue, was established as Guilin Commandery in the Qin Dynasty, named for its lush osmanthus forests. Since Emperor Qin Shi Huang built the Lingqu Canal in 214 AD, Guilin became a cultural crossroads between Lingnan and Central China. By the Tang and Song dynasties, it was known as the 'Southwest Metropolis,' attracting scholars and poets. During the Ming and Qing, as the capital of Guangxi, it flourished in commerce and handicrafts, nurturing unique culinary and craft traditions. In modern times, Guilin rice noodles and Sanhua liquor gained fame as local icons. Although no specific founding year exists, the collective of Guilin time-honored brands preserves a millennium of cultural heritage, with techniques and flavors passed down through generations, reflecting the pride and perseverance of Guilin's 'finest landscapes under heaven.'

Cultural Note

Guilin's time-honored techniques often originate from folk traditions. For instance, rice noodle making emphasizes 'white noodles, savory broth, and aromatic marinade,' while Sanhua liquor requires 'three steamings and three flower formations.' These methods blend Central Plains and Lingnan culinary wisdom, showcasing Guilin's respect for and utilization of natural gifts.
