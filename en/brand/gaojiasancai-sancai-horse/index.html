Brand Story

The craft of Gao's Sancai dates back to the Tang Dynasty (618-907 AD), when Tang Sancai, as burial objects, flourished in the Luoyang area. The ancestors of the Gao family were artisans who fired Sancai during the Tang Dynasty, passing down the skill through generations, surviving the Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties. Around 1900, at the end of the Qing Dynasty and the beginning of the Republic of China, Gao Liangtian, a descendant of the Gao family, revived the family craft in Nanshishan Village, Mengjin, Luoyang. He meticulously studied the glazes and forms of ancient Sancai, bringing the long-lost technique back to life. Gao's Sancai features hand-molded bodies and double firing, with glazes that flow naturally, creating a riot of colors, primarily yellow, green, and white, with occasional blue and purple. The works include traditional subjects such as horses, camels, and figures, with vivid shapes and lustrous glazes, reflecting the pinnacle of Tang sculpture and ceramic art. Gao's Sancai is not only a craft inheritance but also a continuation of Tang cultural spirit, carrying historical memory and folk wisdom, and is hailed as a 'treasure of Oriental art.' Today, while preserving ancient methods, Gao's Sancai incorporates modern aesthetics, making it a unique ceramic art representative among China's time-honored brands.

Cultural Note

Gao's Sancai uses local kaolin clay, fired twice: first bisque, then glaze. The glaze is made from quartz and lead oxide, with metal oxides like copper, iron, and cobalt added for color. The technique's hallmark is the fluidity of the glaze, which, through controlled temperature and kiln placement, creates natural dripping and blending effects. Culturally, Sancai reflects the Tang Dynasty's elaborate burial customs, mirroring the era's aesthetics, religion, and social hierarchy, and holds immense historical and artistic value.
