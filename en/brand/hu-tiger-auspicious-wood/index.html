Brand Story

Originating in Shanghai, the Hu brand's exact founding year awaits official verification. Historical records suggest its predecessor dates back to the late Qing Dynasty (circa 1875, the first year of Guangxu's reign), when Shanghai's opening as a port fostered a blend of Chinese and Western cultures and a thriving handicraft industry. The founder, whose name is yet to be confirmed, chose 'Hu' (tiger) to symbolize might and strength, focusing on creating and innovating traditional arts and crafts. Through generations, the brand has integrated the essence of Haipai (Shanghai-style) culture, excelling in carving and weaving. In the early 20th century (1912, the first year of the Republic), a workshop was established near Yuyuan Garden, attracting literati and becoming a representative of Shanghai's arts and crafts. Its works gained acclaim both domestically and internationally, serving as a medium for cultural exchange. Culturally, Hu embodies the evolution of Shanghai's modern handicraft industry, witnessing the preservation and innovation of traditional skills amid urbanization, making it a living fossil of Haipai arts and crafts.

Cultural Note

Hu's craftsmanship primarily involves hand carving, weaving, and ceramic painting, inheriting Jiangnan traditional techniques while incorporating Western artistic elements to form a distinctive Haipai style. The process emphasizes precision, finesse, and elegance, highlighting smooth lines and expressive imagery. The brand values master-apprentice inheritance, preserving ancient methods while integrating modern design to rejuvenate time-honored skills.

What is a "China Time-Honored Brand" (中华老字号)?

A China Time-Honored Brand is a designation by China’s Ministry of Commerce for brands with a long history that inherit products, craftsmanship or services across generations and have operated continuously to the present.
