Brand Story

Located within Beihai Park in Beijing, Fangshan's history dates back to 1925 (the 14th year of the Republic of China). At that time, chefs from the Qing imperial kitchen, including Zhao Renzhai and Sun Shaoran, opened a tea house on the north bank of Beihai Park to make a living. They named it 'Fangshan,' meaning 'imitating the imperial kitchen,' as they prepared dishes following the methods of the imperial cuisine. These chefs had served in the Qing court and were well-versed in imperial culinary techniques, so Fangshan's dishes retained the authentic flavors of the palace. In 1956, after the founding of the People's Republic of China, Fangshan Restaurant was formally established and moved to the Yilan Hall on Qionghua Island. Fangshan specializes in imperial dishes such as the 'Manchu-Han Imperial Banquet,' 'Pea Flour Cake,' and 'Kidney Bean Rolls,' known for their meticulous preparation, premium ingredients, and unique flavors. The establishment of Fangshan not only continued the imperial culinary tradition but also added a distinctive historical depth to Beijing's dining scene.

Cultural Note

Fangshan's culinary techniques originate from the Qing imperial kitchen, emphasizing the finest ingredients, generous seasoning, and precise cooking times, resulting in rich flavors that highlight the natural taste of the ingredients. Its pastries, such as Pea Flour Cake and Kidney Bean Rolls, require multiple intricate steps. Many of Fangshan's inheritors are descendants of imperial chefs, passing down their skills through generations. Fangshan has also served national leaders, including Premier Zhou Enlai. Its cultural value lies in preserving the complete form of imperial cuisine, making it an important representative of Beijing's food culture.
