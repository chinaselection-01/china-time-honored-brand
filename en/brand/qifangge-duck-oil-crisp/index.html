Brand Story

Qifangge was founded in the late Qing Dynasty and early Republic of China, with no exact year recorded. It is said to have originated as a teahouse near Confucius Temple in Nanjing, and through generations of careful management, it evolved into a renowned eatery offering both tea and snacks. During the Republic era, Qifangge gained fame for its duck-oil crispy sesame cakes and assorted vegetable buns, becoming a gathering place for literati and common folk alike. Its name, meaning 'rare delicacies that leave a lingering fragrance in the mouth,' reflects a pursuit of culinary excellence. The rise and fall of Qifangge is closely tied to the changes of Nanjing, witnessing the prosperity and vicissitudes of the Confucius Temple area. Its traditional techniques, such as the flaky layering of duck-oil cakes and the blending of fillings, follow ancient methods passed down through generations, making it an important carrier of Jinling culinary culture. Despite lacking an officially recognized founding year, its status and influence in Nanjing's culinary history make it an indispensable part of China's time-honored brand heritage.

Cultural Note

The traditional snack-making techniques of Qifangge, especially the baking process of duck-oil crispy cakes, emphasize the coordination of heat and skill, reflecting the refinement and pragmatism of Jinling cuisine. Its business style and dishes are deeply influenced by the market culture along the Qinhuai River, serving as a living fossil for studying modern Nanjing social life and dietary customs.
