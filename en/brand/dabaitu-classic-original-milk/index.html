Brand Story

White Rabbit milk candy traces its origins back to 1943, when Feng Boyong of Shanghai's Aipixi Candy Factory created it. Inspired by the British 'nougat' candy, Feng combined Western techniques with traditional Chinese confectionery to craft a creamy, aromatic candy, naming it 'White Rabbit' to symbolize liveliness and charm. In the 1950s, the factory merged into Shanghai Guanshengyuan Food Factory, making White Rabbit its flagship product. In 1972, Premier Zhou Enlai presented White Rabbit candies to U.S. President Nixon as a state gift, earning it the title of 'sweet envoy' of Sino-American diplomacy. Since then, it has become a national sensation, evoking childhood nostalgia for generations. The iconic blue-white-red packaging, featuring a rabbit design by the Shanghai Art & Design Company, is deeply ingrained in popular culture. White Rabbit is not just a candy; it is a symbol of China's modern industry and national brands, witnessing the evolution of Shanghai's and China's food industry.

Cultural Note

The craftsmanship of White Rabbit candy blends Chinese and Western confectionery techniques, using traditional methods like open-flame sugar boiling and hand-pulling to ensure its chewy texture. Its packaging design, a hallmark of its era, has become an icon of nostalgia. White Rabbit has also expanded into ice cream, milk tea, and other cross-industry products, showcasing the innovative vitality of this time-honored brand in the modern era.
