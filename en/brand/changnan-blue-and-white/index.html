Brand Story

Changnan, today's Jingdezhen in Jiangxi Province, was historically known as Changnan Town, named for its location south of the Chang River. Its porcelain-making history dates back to the Han Dynasty and flourished by the Tang Dynasty, producing lustrous wares that gained fame across the land. During the Jingde era of the Song Dynasty, Emperor Zhenzong bestowed the name 'Jingdezhen,' yet the name Changnan endured in legacy. Changnan porcelain is famed for being 'white as jade, bright as a mirror, thin as paper, and resonant as a chime.' Its craftsmanship synthesizes the finest techniques, especially blue-and-white, famille rose, and colored glazes, which are treasured both at home and abroad. In the Ming and Qing dynasties, the imperial kiln factory was established here to produce porcelain exclusively for the royal court. Changnan porcelain became a significant symbol of Chinese culture, spreading across Asia, Africa, and Europe via the Maritime Silk Road, profoundly influencing global ceramic development. Changnan is not merely a place name but a symbol of artisan spirit, embodying the wisdom and dedication of countless potters and witnessing the glorious journey of Chinese ceramics from inception to zenith.

Cultural Note

Changnan's porcelain-making techniques involve dozens of processes, including raw material preparation, shaping, glazing, and firing, with hand-throwing, trimming, and painting as the core skills. Its traditional wood-fired kiln technique relies on the artisan's experience to control the heat, producing glazes that are warm and lustrous, with the saying 'one color in the kiln, myriad colors out.' Changnan's ceramic art has been passed down through generations, forming a unique master-apprentice system and integrating calligraphy, painting, and poetry, making it a vital carrier of traditional Chinese culture.
