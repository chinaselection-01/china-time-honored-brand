Brand Story

Huangjinxiang originates from Xiamen, a city that has been a vital port on the Maritime Silk Road since the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644), where jewelry trade flourished. During the Qing Dynasty (1644-1912), Xiamen boasted skilled goldsmiths and silversmiths, and Huangjinxiang is believed to be among these craftsmen, renowned for creating gold and silver jewelry and setting precious stones. The brand name means 'precious as gold, fragrance spreading far,' embodying the Minnan merchants' tradition of integrity and quality. In modern times, as Xiamen opened to foreign trade, Huangjinxiang may have incorporated Nanyang (Southeast Asian) styles, blending Chinese and Western techniques. However, due to a lack of systematic records, the brand's exact lineage and key figures await verification. Huangjinxiang carries cultural significance as a testament to Xiamen's role as a hometown of overseas Chinese and the continuity of Minnan jewelry craftsmanship. Its products are often used in weddings and festivals, symbolizing good fortune and prosperity, and are cherished by locals. Further research is needed to uncover the brand's true history.

Cultural Note

Huangjinxiang's craftsmanship inherits the traditional Minnan gold and silver fine work, including chasing, filigree, and setting, with an emphasis on handcrafted precision. Its style blends Central Plains and maritime cultures, featuring auspicious motifs such as dragons, phoenixes, peonies, and bats. The brand upholds the use of genuine materials and meticulous workmanship, reflecting the Minnan merchants' business philosophy of integrity.
