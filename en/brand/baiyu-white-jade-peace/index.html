Brand Story

Baiyu's origins trace back to the imperial jade workshops of the Qing Dynasty. During the Qianlong reign (1736-1795), the court established jade workshops that gathered the finest craftsmen from across the empire to create jade artifacts exclusively for the royal family. These artisans, known for their exceptional skill and refined style, laid the foundation for Beijing's jade carving tradition. After the fall of the Qing Dynasty (1912-1949), these imperial craftsmen dispersed, with some settling in Beijing to open their own workshops, preserving and passing on their court techniques. It was in this context that Baiyu was founded by several master jade carvers who upheld the principles of 'selecting fine materials, carving with precision, and imbuing deep meaning.' Using precious stones like Hetian jade and jadeite, Baiyu's creations range from ornamental pieces to wearable accessories, beloved by collectors. The brand not only continues the artistry and aesthetics of imperial jade carving but also integrates the philosophical ideals of jade culture—such as 'gentle as jade' and 'virtue like jade'—into modern life, making it a vital inheritor of Chinese jade heritage.

Cultural Note

Baiyu carries forward the Beijing jade carving tradition, characterized by 'tailoring the design to the natural form and color of the material.' Techniques include relief carving, openwork carving, and round carving, resulting in fluid lines and vivid forms. The brand adheres to traditional handcrafting, blending imperial artistry with folk aesthetics, embodying the cultural maxim: 'Jade must have workmanship, workmanship must have meaning, and meaning must be auspicious.'
