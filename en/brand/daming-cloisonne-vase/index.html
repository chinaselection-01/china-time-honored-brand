Brand Story

Daming, a craft brand sharing its name with the city of Beijing, traces its origins to the Ming Dynasty, symbolizing light and splendor. Although its founding year awaits official verification, legend holds that its techniques date back to Ming imperial artisans, later spreading to the folk. The brand is renowned for exquisite artworks, including cloisonné, jade carvings, and lacquerware. Key figures are the generations of craftsmen who, through oral instruction, blended courtly skills with folk aesthetics, forging a distinctive Beijing style. Daming's creations are not just functional objects but epitomes of traditional Chinese beauty. In modern times, Daming pieces have adorned significant venues, witnessing historical shifts. Yet, due to scattered historical records, specific figures and events remain to be verified, and the brand's story continues to evolve.

Cultural Note

Daming's craftsmanship merges imperial and folk techniques, emphasizing material quality and intricate workmanship, reflecting Beijing's regional culture. Its production processes are complex—cloisonné, for instance, involves dozens of steps like body-making, wire-inlaying, and enamel-filling, preserving ancient artisans' wisdom. The brand upholds the principle that 'every craft carries meaning, and every meaning brings auspiciousness,' with motifs of fortune, longevity, and peace, serving as tangible carriers of Chinese auspicious culture.
