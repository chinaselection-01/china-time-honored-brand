Brand Story

Ding Lianfang, a celebrated snack brand from Huzhou, Zhejiang, traces its origins to the Guangxu era of the Qing Dynasty (around 1878). Founder Ding Lianfang initially traded in silk wadding before venturing into the food business. Setting up a stall in front of Huzhou's City God Temple, he pioneered triangular wontons filled with fresh pork, bamboo shoot tips, and sesame. With thin wrappers and generous fillings, the wontons were savory and delicious, quickly winning public favor. Known for quality ingredients and meticulous craftsmanship, the brand gained fame as a local delicacy. Beyond food, Ding Lianfang wontons carry the culinary culture and street memories of Huzhou's water towns, witnessing the commercial prosperity of modern Jiangnan cities. The craft has been passed down through generations, preserving traditional flavors and making it a vital representative of Zhejiang's food culture.

Cultural Note

The making of Ding Lianfang wontons emphasizes 'Three Fresh' (fresh meat, fresh bamboo shoots, fresh broth). The wrappers are hand-rolled to a delicate thinness, and fillings adjust with the seasons. The brand's commitment to genuine ingredients reflects the integrity tradition of Jiangnan merchants. In 2011, its craft was inscribed in the Zhejiang Provincial Intangible Cultural Heritage list, making it a living fossil of Huzhou's culinary culture.
