Brand Story

Qihua traces its roots to Jiangsu, a region renowned for lacquerware since ancient times. Yangzhou lacquerware gained fame as early as the Han Dynasty, and by the Ming and Qing dynasties, Jiangsu's lacquer craftsmanship had reached its zenith. Although the brand's official founding year is not documented, historical records suggest its predecessor was a lacquer workshop dating back to the mid-Qing Dynasty. The name 'Qihua' (literally 'lacquer flower') symbolizes the blossoming of traditional craftsmanship. A key figure in its development was Liang Guohai, a master lacquer artist of the Republican era who blended northern and southern techniques, establishing Qihua's distinctive style. After the founding of the People's Republic of China, Qihua consolidated several workshops through public-private partnership, becoming a flagship of Jiangsu's lacquer industry. Qihua pieces have been presented as state gifts to foreign dignitaries, embodying the aesthetic sensibilities of Jiangnan literati and the refined, understated beauty of Eastern art.

Cultural Note

Qihua's craftsmanship encompasses carved lacquer (diaoqi), mother-of-pearl inlay (dianluo), and painted lacquer (caihui), with carved lacquer being the most celebrated. The process involves over a hundred steps, including base-making, lacquer coating, carving, and polishing, often taking months to complete. Qihua works integrate painting, carving, and inlay, showcasing Chinese artisans' profound understanding and masterful use of natural materials.
