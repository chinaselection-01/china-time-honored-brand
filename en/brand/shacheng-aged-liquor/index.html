Brand Story

Shacheng, located in Huailai, Hebei, was historically known as Shachengbao, a fortified town established during the Ming Dynasty for border defense. Blessed with fertile land and abundant sorghum and wheat, the region naturally lent itself to winemaking. According to the Huailai County Gazetteer, during the Kangxi reign (1662-1722), several distilleries operated within Shachengbao, producing the renowned 'Shacheng Shaojiu' (burnt wine). By the Guangxu era (1875-1908), the Wang family, local master brewers, refined the process using the 'Lao Wu Zeng' method, yielding a clear, mellow liquor that was sold far and wide in Beijing and Tianjin. During the Republic period, over a dozen distilleries flourished, creating a landscape dotted with wine banners. After the founding of New China, the Shacheng Distillery was established through public-private partnership, preserving ancient methods and developing the iconic 'Shacheng Laojiao' series. The brand carries the historical memory of frontier farming and trade, blending northern ruggedness with finesse, making it a vital symbol of Hebei's culinary culture.

Cultural Note

Shacheng's brewing techniques follow ancient traditions, emphasizing low-temperature fermentation and slow maturation, with century-old pit mud nurturing unique microbial communities. The process highlights 'watching the bubbles to select the wine and grading for storage,' showcasing the mastery of northern distilled spirits. Shacheng's wine culture merges frontier boldness with agricultural wisdom, serving as a centerpiece for local festivals and hospitality.
