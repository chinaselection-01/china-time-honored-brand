Brand Story

Zhang Ji traces its origins to the mid-Qing Dynasty (around 1800) in Shaanxi, founded by the Zhang family's ancestor, a renowned local chef from Guanzhong. Known for exquisite culinary skills and honest business practices, the brand opened its first shop in Xi'an during the Daoguang reign (1821-1850), serving local snacks that quickly gained popularity. In the Republic era (1912-1949), the third-generation heir Zhang Defu innovated traditional recipes, elevating the brand's reputation. After 1949, Zhang Ji navigated through socialist transformation and reform, preserving traditional techniques while embracing modern management. Now led by the sixth-generation heir Zhang Mingyuan, Zhang Ji remains dedicated to promoting and innovating Shaanxi cuisine, carrying forward a legacy that embodies regional culture and historical change.

Cultural Note

Zhang Ji's century-old techniques adhere to ancient methods, such as the secret broth for Hulutou and the aged marinade for La Zhi Rou, following ancestral recipes. The intricate preparation, emphasizing heat and timing, reflects Shaanxi cuisine's style of 'ruggedness with finesse.' Zhang Ji also highlights natural flavors, integrating Guanzhong food culture, making it a living testament to Shaanxi folk customs and culinary evolution.
