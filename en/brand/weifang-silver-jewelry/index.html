Brand Story

Weifang, historically known as Weixian, is located on the Shandong Peninsula and has been a thriving commercial hub since the Ming and Qing dynasties, with a well-developed handicraft industry. The jewelry sector here has deep roots, with local artisans passing down gold and silver craftsmanship through generations, blending the profound Qilu culture with the exquisite folk customs of Jiaodong. Although the brand lacks an officially verified founding year, local chronicles and industry reputation trace its techniques back to the mid-to-late Qing Dynasty, when Weixian was dotted with silver workshops. Artisans excelled in techniques such as chasing (zanke), filigree (huasi), and setting (xiangqian), crafting ornaments for officials and for folk weddings. In modern times, Weifang jewelry has integrated contemporary design concepts while preserving tradition, resulting in pieces that balance classical elegance with a modern touch. Guided by the principles of 'integrity first, craftsmanship as the soul,' the brand strives to explore and promote Weifang's regional culture. Its works often draw inspiration from local elements like Weifang kites and Yangjiabu New Year paintings, earning it a reputation as a cultural ambassador of Qilu. Although specific founding details and corporate information are yet to be confirmed, the brand's role in preserving local culture has been recognized, and it is actively applying for intangible cultural heritage status to continue its century-old legacy.

Cultural Note

The brand's techniques are rooted in Weifang's traditional gold and silver craftsmanship, encompassing chasing, filigree, setting, and enamel work, with a particular strength in hand-chased patterns. The craft emphasizes precision, steadiness, and uniformity, with motifs often drawn from folk auspicious symbols and local scenery, reflecting the aesthetic of central Shandong. Each piece undergoes dozens of manual steps, highlighting detail and texture. The brand is committed to preserving endangered techniques through apprenticeships and collaborations with local universities, fostering the integration of traditional craft with modern design.
