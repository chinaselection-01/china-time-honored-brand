Brand Story

Jinsha Jewelry traces its origins to Guizhou, where the silver-making skills of local ethnic minorities, such as the Miao and Dong, were already highly refined during the Ming and Qing dynasties. According to legend, during the Qianlong reign (1736-1795), silversmiths in the Jinsha area crafted exquisite silver ornaments for local chieftains and nobles, gradually developing a distinctive style. In the early 20th century, with the revival of ethnic handicrafts, the Jinsha brand was formally established, dedicated to merging traditional Miao silver techniques with modern aesthetics. The founder, Mr. [Pending Verification], learned silver crafting from a young age and became deeply knowledgeable about Miao totems and patterns. He led his team across Guizhou's villages, collecting and documenting traditional motifs while innovatively incorporating modern design concepts. Jinsha Jewelry preserves ancient techniques such as chasing, filigree, and knotting, while infusing contemporary minimalist styles, resulting in pieces that are both culturally resonant and fashion-forward. The brand's products are beloved by consumers both domestically and internationally, serving as a vital medium for spreading Guizhou's ethnic culture.

Cultural Note

The core craftsmanship of Jinsha Jewelry originates from the Miao silver forging technique, which has been inscribed on the National Intangible Cultural Heritage list. The brand insists on handmade production, with each piece undergoing dozens of processes including silver melting, forging, chasing, welding, and polishing, reflecting the Miao people's pursuit of beauty and cultural continuity. Additionally, the brand emphasizes the integration of traditional craftsmanship with modern design, breathing new life into ancient skills.
